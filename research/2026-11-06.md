## Bank of England raises Bank Rate to 4% (UK)

- **Country:** UK
- **Category:** Interest rates
- **What changed:** On 5 November 2026 the Monetary Policy Committee raised Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, from 3.75% to 4%. It had been held at 3.75% in June, July and September. The next decision is 17 December 2026.
  - Reported (lead): the vote was 8-1, with one member wanting a bigger move to 4.25%. Also reported: it's the first rise since August 2023, and the Governor signalled more increases are likely. The official summary page hadn't loaded the text when it was fetched today, so none of this is verified.
- **What it means for a viewer:** UK tracker mortgages and other variable-rate borrowing get more expensive straight away. Easy-access savings rates usually follow, but banks often pass on only part of a rise, so savers should check whether their own rate actually moved.
- **Status:** verified (4%, the 0.25-point rise on 5 Nov, the next date of 17 Dec). Lead: the vote split, the "first rise since 2023" framing and the guidance.
- **Sources:** https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/the-interest-rate-bank-rate · lead: https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/bank-of-england-rate-rise-november-2026-mortgage
- **Short angle:** This completes Timely idea #1 ("Rates are turning up again"). All four banks have now moved toward tighter policy: the BoE (4%), the Bank of Canada (2.5%) and the RBI (5.5%) have hiked, and the Fed held at 3.75-4% with two votes for a hike. A worked-math Short could show what a 0.25-point rise adds to a variable-rate balance in each country, and point out that savings rates don't move up automatically.

## Wealthsimple Presents: new products announced (Canada)

- **Country:** Canada
- **Category:** Banks and fintechs
- **What changed:** The Nov 5 event page now lists what launched:
  - **Available today:** savings accounts at 2.5% interest; kids and teens accounts (parental controls, a card, up to 2.25% interest); Wealthsimple Pay (send up to $100,000 instantly to other Wealthsimple users); $0-commission CAD options; direct indexing; a portfolio line of credit (no rate listed).
  - **Coming this month:** perpetual futures (described as a first for retail traders in Canada; waitlist).
  - **Coming late 2026:** a CAD-backed stablecoin ("CADD") with 3.25% APY in rewards; virtual credit cards with their own expiry dates and spend limits.
  - **Coming early 2027:** 24/7 US stock trading; an AI financial assistant; connections to Claude and ChatGPT; a rebuilt app; a business credit card with 1% cash back.
  - **Promotion:** a November "Monthly Millionaire" contest; opening and funding a savings account gives 10,000 entries. Terms apply.
- **What it means for a viewer:** For Canadians, the 2.5% savings rate is the headline everyday number, and it now sits at the Bank of Canada's 2.5% policy rate. Most of the rest is aimed at active traders. Perpetual futures and the stablecoin are high-risk or untested products, so they need careful framing if they're ever covered.
- **Status:** verified (on the official event page fetched today). Fees, eligibility and terms for each product weren't checked; check them on the product pages before scripting.
- **Sources:** https://www.wealthsimple.com/en-ca/presents/something-good
- **Short angle:** Canada only, so it's weak for this audience. A possible money-skills Short: "2.5% interest: is it beating inflation?" Only make it after checking the savings account's terms page and the latest Canadian CPI. Don't promote leveraged products.

## Emirates Skywards: new miles expiry rule from 1 June 2027 (worldwide)

- **Country:** Worldwide (program-level)
- **Category:** Loyalty programs
- **What changed:** Emirates' official program-updates page says miles earned from 1 June 2027 will be valid for three years from the flight or partner activity date, rounded to the end of that month. Today, miles expire three years after they're earned, rounded to the end of the member's birth month. Miles already in an account aren't affected. Not on the watch list, and the change was first announced earlier (reported in August); recorded because earlier runs didn't have it.
  - The page's worked example has an inconsistent date (it applies the new rule to miles earned in 2024), so quote only the rule itself.
- **What it means for a viewer:** Emirates miles still expire on a fixed three-year clock, and activity doesn't extend them. From mid-2027 the expiry date depends on when you earned the miles, not on your birthday.
- **Status:** verified (the rule and the 1 June 2027 date). The page doesn't say whether any activity extends validity; don't claim it does.
- **Sources:** https://www.emirates.com/ch/english/skywards/emirates-skywards-program-updates/
- **Short angle:** Possible add-on for the idea bank's "points expiry: how to keep miles alive" (contrast: Aeroplan's 18-month inactivity rule versus Emirates' hard three-year clock). Not urgent.
