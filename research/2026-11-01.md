## Southwest Rapid Rewards overhaul for 2027

- **Country:** US
- **Category:** Loyalty programs / credit cards
- **What changed:** Southwest's 2027 program page (announced Oct 15, 2026) sets out these dates:
  - **Dec 31, 2026:** Companion Pass qualifying points are retired.
  - **Jan 1, 2027:** A-List becomes Silver (35,000 tier qualifying points or 25 flights, 40% earning bonus), A-List Preferred becomes Gold (65,000 or 50 flights, 60% bonus), and a new Platinum tier arrives (140,000 or 100 flights, 120% bonus, unlimited Companion Pass trips). New milestone rewards at 20,000 and 95,000 points.
  - **Jan 1, 2027:** every Rapid Rewards credit card earns 1 tier qualifying point per $1 of purchases. During 2027, cardmembers who reach 130,000 get a 10,000-point boost.
  - **Spring 2027:** earning and redeeming points with Icelandair, China Airlines and Etihad Airways, plus points for seat selection and rental cars.
  
  The page lists no price changes for existing Southwest awards.
- **What it means for a viewer:** US Southwest flyers can now earn status from card spending alone, and the program gains its first international partners. If you were chasing a Companion Pass with this year's rules, the qualifying points you earn stop counting after Dec 31.
- **Status:** verified
- **Sources:** https://www.southwest.com/rapid-rewards/2027/ · lead (announcement date): https://wsau.com/2026/10/15/southwest-revamps-loyalty-program-with-new-premium-tiers/
- **Short angle:** Weak for this channel. It's US-only, and status-from-spending isn't a points-value story. Possibly later: "Book Etihad with Southwest points?" once spring 2027 partner pricing is published.

## Amex India: more Nov 15 changes (late fees, milestones, service outages)

- **Country:** India
- **Category:** Credit cards
- **What changed:** The Amex India updates page now lists more changes on top of the points split already recorded on Oct 31:
  - From Nov 15, 2026, late fees run from ₹500 to ₹1,250 and are set by the Total Amount Due instead of the Minimum Amount Due (credit cards only).
  - From Nov 15, milestone spending counts by posting date, not purchase date, and milestone benefits are credited by the end of the month you qualify.
  - If a payment is still due at your next statement, Membership Rewards earning and redemption pause on that card until you pay.
  - Points can be redeemed for statement credit in the new app, with a minimum of 1,000 points.
  - Services are disrupted from Nov 11 to 18, including online card cancellation (Nov 12-15) and post-purchase EMI (Nov 11-15). Payments may take longer to show from Nov 12 to 16.
- **What it means for a viewer:** Indian Amex members should combine points onto the card they'll redeem from, and pay any balance, before Nov 11 rather than Nov 15. The outage window means last-minute moves may not go through.
- **Status:** verified
- **Sources:** https://www.americanexpress.com/en-in/support/digital/consumer-experience-updates/
- **Short angle:** None new. `videos/amex-points-split-by-card.mp4` already covers the split. If a follow-up is made, the hook is the earlier effective deadline: outages start Nov 11.

## ICICI Bank's official currency-conversion (DCC) fee schedule

- **Country:** India
- **Category:** Credit cards / money of travel
- **What changed:** Nothing new. ICICI's upcoming-changes page confirms the DCC fees in force since the Jan 15, 2026 statement: 3.50% on most ICICI cards (including the MakeMyTrip Signature and Platinum cards), 2.00% on Emeralde, 1.99% on Amazon Pay ICICI, 1.49% on Times Black and 0.99% on the MakeMyTrip ICICI Bank Travel card.
- **What it means for a viewer:** On most ICICI cards, choosing to pay in rupees at a foreign terminal costs a 3.5% bank fee, before the merchant's own exchange-rate markup. It's a real Indian number that backs up the existing DCC Short.
- **Status:** verified
- **Sources:** https://www.icici.bank.in/personal-banking/cards/credit-card/upcoming-changes-features-and-charges
- **Short angle:** None now (`videos/dcc-pay-local-currency.mp4` exists). Use it as an India example in a future long-form or a follow-up on DCC.
